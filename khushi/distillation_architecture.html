1 · Inputs per training sample

Four tensors are read per tile, all (B, 3, 256, 256) in [-1, 1]. The teacher and student are fed different band stacks on purpose — the teacher was trained on true colour, the student must live in LISS-4's band space.

Why netG2 needs a head

The student holds one feature per depth. Demanding it equal netG's fused feature and netG2's pure-radar feature is contradictory — the trunk would split the difference and learn neither. A prediction head changes the requirement from “your feature must be the radar view” to “your feature must contain enough to derive it”, which is the honest form of the claim and is learnable.

Each UnetSkipConnectionBlock returns cat([x, model(x)]), so one forward hook yields both halves: the encoder activation is the hook's input, the decoder activation is the tail of its output. The 512 × 1 × 1 bottleneck appears in no block output and needs its own hook on the innermost block's model[1].

4 · What “compared” means at each tap

TermOperationComparesSurvives band + resolution shiftWeight
heatmap Square, average over channels, L2-normalise → collapses the channel axis 1 × H × W yes 1.0
raw Direct match through a learned 1×1 Conv2d adapter C × H × W no — adapter is fitted to S2 0.3

The heatmap term leads because collapsing the channel axis makes it immune to the two mismatches this project has: the teacher's blue channel vs the student's infrared, and Sentinel-2's 10 m vs LISS-4's 5.8 m. The raw term keeps value-level detail at a weight low enough that its Sentinel-2-fitted adapters don't dictate the solution.

The property that must hold

I_g must remain a pure function of I_c. Everything in the right-hand column above is scaffolding that shapes the trunk during training and is then thrown away. What ships is 108.8 M parameters that take a cloudy 3-band image and return a clear one — with the radar knowledge baked into the weights rather than needing a radar image at runtime.
